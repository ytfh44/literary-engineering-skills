# Knowledge Boundaries

## Use when

The plot's engine is not what happens but who knows what when. Scenes that
depend on information asymmetry — a character acting on a misbelief, a
reader knowing what the character does not, a secret whose holder is
unclear — need their knowledge map made explicit.

## Do not use when

Do not add mystery for its own sake. Withholding is only meaningful when
the reader can reconstruct what is being withheld and why. Do not confuse
knowledge with phrasing: what a character can know (knowledge boundaries)
is not the same as how they phrase it (see `dialogue-voice`).

## Core test

**For the current scene, draw the knowledge map. Whose knowledge differs
from whose, and does every difference do work?**

If two parties hold different beliefs and nothing in the scene or later
scene consumes the difference, the asymmetry is decoration — or a leak
waiting to happen.

## The map

For each relevant party (characters, narrator, reader), record:

- **facts known** — what they hold as true;
- **beliefs and misbeliefs** — what they hold that is false, and why they
  hold it;
- **knowledge dates** — when each fact was acquired (a fact learned at the
  end cannot motivate an earlier scene);
- **blind spots** — what they have no route to know;
- **suspicion without confirmation** — the gap between guessing and
  knowing.

## Asymmetry types

- **character < character** — one knows what the other does not: the fuel
  of scenes (secrets, plans, threats);
- **character < reader** — the reader knows more than the character:
  dramatic irony, dread;
- **reader < character** — the character knows more than the reader:
  withheld exposition, reveals;
- **narrator < reader or character** — a limited narrator who genuinely
  does not know: the narration's own knowledge boundary.

Each asymmetry type has a different payment schedule. Irony pays at the
moment the character's ignorance costs them; withheld exposition pays at
the reveal; a limited narrator pays by never cheating.

## Workflow

1. List the facts the scene depends on.
2. For each fact, mark who knows it, when they learned it, and whether
   they believe it correctly.
3. Draw the asymmetry that matters for the scene.
4. Check the misbelief's origin: what evidence supports it? A misbelief
   with no origin is a plot convenience.
5. Check the timing: could this party actually know this now?
6. Decide where the payment happens: irony, reveal, or dread — and make
   sure the scene (or a later one) collects it.
7. Repair leaks: if the reader can infer that a character must have known
   something inconsistent with their behavior, the boundary is broken.

## Minimal pair

> 他不知道妻子已经知道了。
>
> 他不知道妻子已经看到了那张收据。

The first names an asymmetry; the second names the evidence that makes the
asymmetry real. Without the evidence, the reader cannot hold the irony;
with it, every later scene between them carries the charge.

> 他以为门锁着。
>
> 他以为门锁着——他从里面锁的。

The second is a knowledge-boundary repair: the misbelief has an origin
(the character's own action). The reader now knows both the misbelief and
its cause, and the door's actual state becomes the scene's question.

## Counterexamples

- A detective's recap may legitimately state facts the reader already
  knows; the recap is for the character's map, not the reader's.
- A child character may hold a misbelief the adults share (family myth);
  the shared misbelief is the unit, not the individual.
- A limited narrator may be wrong about a fact; the narrator's boundary is
  a feature when the work never secretly corrects it.

## Rule strength

**Low — structure tests.** No mystery quotas, no "withhold the twist"
rules, no mandatory reveals. The reliable operations are map drawing
(who knows what when), origin checks (why does this misbelief exist),
timing checks (could they know this now), and payment scheduling (where
does the asymmetry pay). Every rule here is a hypothesis about the reader's
information state.

## Return shape

Return **fact -> known by whom since when -> asymmetry type -> payment
scene -> leak check**. For revision, move knowledge events (learning,
disclosure, concealment) before changing their phrasing.
