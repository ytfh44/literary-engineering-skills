# Dialogue Voice

## Use when

A character says a proposition they may plausibly understand, but in words
they would not plausibly own — or the character's voice needs to be built
(or rebuilt) as a coherent profile across all language layers.

## Do not use when

Do not equate youth, low education, dialect, or informal speech with weak
reasoning. A child can infer sharply; a mechanic can think abstractly; a
professor can swear. The issue is ownership of the sentence, not prestige.
Do not add dialect or slang as decoration; voice is profile, not costume.

## Core test

**Who would naturally own this sentence?**

If the best answer is "the author explaining the character," move the
proposition out of the character's mouth or rebuild it in the character's
operational vocabulary.

## Voice is a cross-layer profile

A character's voice is not one register knob. It is the intersection of
choices from every layer of this pack:

- **preferred names and address terms** — who the character calls 李叔 vs.
  主任 vs. 老李 (see `naming-and-address-engineering`);
- **hedges and certainty markers** — 大概/可能/应该 vs. 肯定/必然;
- **particles and sentence endings** — 吧/呢/呗/啊, or their absence;
- **relation markers** — does the character state edges explicitly
  (因此/不过/所以) or leave them to event order (see
  `relation-scaffolding`);
- **habitual metaphors** — the source domains this character actually lives
  in (fishing, trading, machinery, sports) (see `metaphor-audit`);
- **verb commitment** — does the character claim agency (我决定/我负责) or
  diffuse it (事情就那样发生了) (see `verb-event-engineering`);
- **ellipsis and repair** — what the character leaves unsaid, and how they
  restart a sentence when pressed;
- **proposition vs. register** — what they know versus how they phrase it.

The profile is the character's grammar of ownership. Two characters with
the same information sound different because every layer allocates
ownership differently.

## Workflow

1. Separate **proposition** from **register**. What does the character
   actually know or want?
2. Mark imported abstractions: therapy terms, management nouns, critic
   language, polished causal summaries.
3. Check age, occupation, local jargon, social relation, stress level, and
   what the character is trying to obtain **now**.
4. Re-express the proposition through nouns and verbs the character handles
   routinely.
5. Keep unusual sophistication when the story has earned it through habit,
   obsession, training, or mimicry.
6. Recheck nearby characters. If all of them use the same relation
   vocabulary, the narrator may be leaking through everyone.
7. For profile work: list the character's choices per layer; change one
   layer at a time and read what the voice becomes.

## Minimal pair

> "我觉得我们需要重新审视一下彼此的边界。"
>
> "你别再拿我钥匙。"

For many characters, both can point to the same conflict. The second owns
an object and an action. The first is right only for a speaker who actually
talks in that conceptual register.

Profile pair:

> 他说话从不带"大概"。要么是事实，要么是他不知道。
>
> "他大概会来。" "不，他会来。"

If the character's hedge profile is "no epistemic hedges," the second
sentence is not emphasis — it is the character refusing the very category
of probability. The voice is the layer choices.

## Counterexamples

- A teenager who spends all day on therapy TikTok may naturally say
  "边界感"; age alone is not a veto.
- A frightened engineer may blurt a precise technical diagnosis because
  technical nouns are their shortest path to action.
- A character may deliberately imitate a leader's speech (mimicry is
  learned register); the imitation is the character, not the leak.
- A professor off-duty may swear; register is context-shaped, not fixed by
  occupation.

## Rule strength

**Discourse tests.** No register blacklists, no age/class/education
stereotypes, no vocabulary-level targets. The reliable operations are
ownership attribution (who owns this sentence), proposition/register
separation, and per-layer profile changes tested one variable at a time.
The profile model is reference, not prescription: any layer choice can be
earned or borrowed by the story.

## Return shape

Return **proposition -> current owner -> likely character owner -> register
repair**, or, for profile work, **layer -> current choice -> tested
alternative -> voice delta**. When revising dialogue, preserve the
character's reasoning even when simplifying diction.
