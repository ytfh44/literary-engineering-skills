# The Voice Profile

## What a profile is

A character's voice is the intersection of choices across language layers.
Build it as a list, test it one layer at a time, and use it to predict what
the character would or would not say.

## Profile fields

1. **Names/address** — who does the character call by name, title, kin
   term, or nickname? What do others call them? (Frame: `naming-and-
   address-engineering`.)
2. **Hedges** — 大概/可能/应该/也许/肯定. Does the character grade
   certainty, and in what direction (toward doubt or toward claim)?
3. **Particles/endings** — 吧/呢/呗/啊/嘛, or none. Attitude markers:
   coaxing, dismissive, resigned, bare.
4. **Relation markers** — does this character state edges (因为/所以/
   但是/其实) or leave them implicit? An analytic character overmarks; a
   scene-dwelling character under-marks.
5. **Habitual domains** — where do this character's comparisons come from?
   The domain list is a biography (see `metaphor-audit` voice axis).
6. **Verb commitment** — agency claims (我决定), patient stances (被),
   impersonal constructions (事情发生了). Who is the character in their own
   sentences?
7. **Ellipsis** — what does the character leave out under pressure? The
   gaps are content.
8. **Repair** — how does the character restart a broken sentence? Repair
   patterns reveal fluency, evasion, and class of training.

## Building and testing

1. Gather 5-10 owned sentences of the character (dialogue or close
   narration).
2. Fill the profile fields from evidence; leave a field empty when there is
   no evidence.
3. Generate a test sentence in the character's voice from the profile.
4. Compare: does it sound like the character? If not, find the layer that
   drifted.
5. Change one layer at a time (one-variable interventions, per
   `counterfactual-revision`) and read the voice delta.

## The narrator-leak test

If all characters share a relation-marker habit, a hedge profile, or a
comparison domain, the narrator is leaking through everyone. Test: strip
the shared layer from every character; if the remaining voices still
differ, the leak was only in that layer.

## Minimal pair

Same proposition, three profiles:

> "我会来的。" (bare commitment)
>
> "我大概会来。" (graded commitment, room to fail)
>
> "来吧，我还能不来吗？" (rhetorical, relational, self-aware)

All three commit to attending. The first owns the decision, the second owns
uncertainty, the third owns the relationship. The profile decides which one
the character can say.

## Counterexamples

- A character with a rich internal voice may speak flatly to strangers;
   profile is context-shaped (audience is a variable).
- Mimicry: a character who imitates a parent, boss, or partner will
   temporarily borrow another profile; the borrowing is the character's
   act, not a leak.
- Growth: a character's profile can shift after a specific event; the shift
   is a story event, not a defect (see `character-motive` on value change).
