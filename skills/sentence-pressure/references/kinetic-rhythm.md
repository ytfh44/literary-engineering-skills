# Kinetic Rhythm: the Prosodic Track

## When to use this track

Semantic pressure asks when information arrives. Kinetic pressure asks how
the sentence moves. Use this reference when the prose sounds wrong spoken
(or subvocal) even though information timing is fine, or when repetition
feels ritual rather than redundant.

## The five levers

1. **Stress beats** — where emphasis lands in a clause. Chinese four-beat
   proverbs and two-plus-two phrasing, English iambic drift, French
   phrase-final stress: every language has default beat patterns, and prose
   that fights them without reason feels heavy.
2. **Breath units** — the chunks a speaker can produce in one breath.
   Overlong units force the reader to hold syntax past comfort; chopped
   units force constant restarting. Neither is wrong by itself; both are
   wrong when they fight the semantic path.
3. **Syntactic momentum** — does the clause chain run forward (subject-verb
   continuation), stall (stacked modifiers), or loop (returning
   constructions)? Momentum is how the sentence feels like it is going
   somewhere.
4. **Parallelism** — repeated structures carry weight when the repetition is
   the point (ritual, incantation, building lists) and march pointlessly
   when it is not. Test: delete one member of the series. If the series
   loses its force, the parallelism was load-bearing.
5. **Prosodic weight** — long open vowels and liquids slow a sentence;
   clusters and monosyllables speed or harden it. 灯亮，灯灭，他走 is hard
   and fast; 他沿着走廊走下去 is continuous. Weight is an instrument, not a
   preference.

## Ritual vs. filler repetition

- **Ritual repetition** changes the work state: each iteration adds force,
   scale, or dread (the third 灯亮,灯灭 is not the first).
- **Filler repetition** repeats without changing anything.

The repetition rule from `literary-strategy-controller` applies here:
repetition must change the work state. In the kinetic track this is judged
by force and weight, not by information.

## Minimal pair

> 雨。雨。雨。
>
> 雨一直下。

The first is three stress beats with nothing between them — the repetition
is the event's weight. The second is a statement about duration. If the
scene is the monotony, the first is exact; if the scene needs the fact of
duration, the second is cheaper. Neither is "more rhythmic."

> 他等。她等。所有人都等着。
>
> 他们在等待中沉默。

The first builds a series that escalates the waiting from two people to
everyone; the parallelism is structural. The second compresses the same
fact into a state description and loses the escalation.

## Counterexamples

- A breathless, run-on narrator voice can be deliberate (panic, intoxication,
  excitement); do not "fix" it into breath units.
- Monosyllabic hard prose can be a character's register; the hardness is
  voice.
- In Chinese, four-beat phrase rhythm is a default pattern; prose that never
  uses it and prose that never breaks it are both mannered in their own
  way. Audit the beat against the scene's needs, not against a rhythm
  ideal.
