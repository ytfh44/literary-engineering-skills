# Utility Axes for Metaphor Audit

## Why axes instead of a score

"Good metaphor" and "bad metaphor" are verdicts that hide the mechanism. The
axes force the audit to say *what the comparison does* and *what it costs*.
The same image can be a failure on one axis and the whole point on another.

## The ten axes

1. **Semantic gain** — does the source add structure, mechanism, or relation
   the target lacked? "他的犹豫像一台卡住的机器" adds a mechanism (jam,
   stall). "她的眼睛像星星" adds nothing but shine.
2. **Perceptual gain** — does it change how the scene is seen, not what it
   is called? Apparent motion, scale surprise, texture that was not visible
   before.
3. **Voice gain** — whose comparison is it? A fisherman says "像收网一样";
   a trader says "像止损一样." The image is evidence about the speaker. A
   stale image owned by the character is voice; the same image owned by the
   narrator is noise.
4. **Rhythmic gain** — does the image carry the sentence's timing? A long
   clause that releases on the comparison; a repeated refrain with weight.
5. **Motif gain** — does it feed an element that returns and changes work
   state? Third occurrence may add zero object-information and still be the
   motif's payoff.
6. **Cultural-memory gain** — an ancient or proverbial comparison (like 蝉翼)
   carries recall weight even with zero novelty. The weight is real; the
   question is whether this scene needs it.
7. **Comic gain** — the mismatch itself is the event. "他的领带像一条
   忠实的狗" fails as description and succeeds as comedy; audit it as comedy.
8. **Estrangement gain** — the familiar deliberately made strange (defamiliar-
   ization). Price: reader must rebuild the reference. Worth it only when the
   estrangement is the point, not the decoration.
9. **Processing cost** — words spent and cognition demanded: the size of the
   source domain the reader must carry into the target scene.
10. **Interference cost** — the image distracts from the scene's actual
    object or steals agency from the event. A metaphor that makes the reader
    watch the image instead of the action is not "vivid," it is a detour.

## Common misdiagnoses

- **"删掉以后事实没变，所以删。"** Wrong when the axis is affective/voice/
  motif: those axes do not add facts by definition.
- **"太老套，所以删。"** Cliché is a cost, not a verdict. A conventional
  comparison can be the cheapest exact carrier; audit what it contributes.
- **"很新奇，所以留。"** Novelty is not an axis. A novel comparison with
  high processing cost and zero structural gain is decoration that spent the
  reader's attention.
- **"有比喻就文学。"** The presence of an image is the weakest proxy for
  literary work. The audit's first possible verdict is "no metaphor here."

## Decision order

1. Classify: which axes are active in this sentence?
2. Sum the gains on active axes; note costs.
3. If gains are all zero and costs are real → delete.
4. If one gain is high and owned (voice/motif/comic) → keep, even when
   semantic gain is zero.
5. If costs exceed gains and no axis is load-bearing → revise or delete;
   do not propose a replacement until the target trait is named.
6. If the sentence needed a verb, viewpoint, or event and the image was
   covering that gap → no new metaphor. Fix the gap.

## Minimal pair

> 他像一块石头一样沉默。 (semantic gain ≈ 0, cost real, no other axis
> active → delete or replace)
>
> 他像一块石头一样沉默，只有喉结在动。 (the second clause makes "石头"
> carry motion-contrast: the image now has a structural job)
>
> 他像一块石头。邻居说，他小时候掉进过井里。 (motif axis: the image
> becomes the story's object; keep and feed it)

The words are almost identical; the axis verdict changes with the job.

## Counterexamples

- A deliberately flat image in an affectless narrator's voice: the flatness
  is the voice (voice gain); do not polish it.
- A pile of stale images in a parody of purple prose: comic gain; audit as
  comedy, not as failed description.
