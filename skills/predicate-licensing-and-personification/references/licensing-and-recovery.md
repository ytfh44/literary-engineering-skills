# Licensing and Recovery

## Predicate requirement inventory

Before judging a crossing, list what the predicate normally demands of its
agent:

- **intention** — the agent wants the result (追, 等待, 决定);
- **control** — the agent can stop or steer (推开, 抓住, 放下);
- **perception** — the agent senses the target (看见, 听见, 认出);
- **motion capacity** — the agent moves under its own power (走, 扑, 逃);
- **volition** — the agent chooses (拒绝, 犹豫, 原谅);
- **sentience** — the agent experiences (知道, 害怕, 记得).

A crossing borrows some subset. The cheapest crossings borrow only what the
scene's geometry already supplies: 雪把声音接住了 borrows a physical
relation (sound is absorbed) without claiming volition. The most expensive
borrow everything and demand the reader re-read the whole sentence.

## Recovery-cost grades

1. **Instant** — the literal event is restored within the same clause
   (风把他往巷子里推: force + direction, no intention claimed).
2. **Cheap** — the scene's next clause confirms the reading (门犹豫了
   一下才开 -> the person behind it is hesitating).
3. **Costly** — the reader must hold the anomaly across sentences or
   consciously forgive it; the crossing is doing decoration duty at
   cognition's expense.
4. **Failed** — an explanation is required after the sentence; the
   crossing's cost exceeds its gain and the sentence should be recast.

## Gain inventory

A crossing is worth its cost when it buys:

- **event structure** — the reclassification creates a consequence (the
  snow's absorption becomes an event the scene uses);
- **viewpoint** — the borrowed perception is the perceiver's (the door's
  hesitation is the character's fear relayed);
- **affect** — the field temperature without a label;
- **motif** — the crossing feeds a repeated mechanism that changes work
  state.

Strangeness alone is not in the inventory.

## Failure patterns

- **The full borrow** — wind that 犹豫着, 决定, and 原谅 all at once:
  every requirement claimed, recovery impossible.
- **The gloss dependency** — "仿佛有什么力量……" after the crossing:
  the sentence reports its own failure.
- **The dead metaphor pile** — 风在叹息, 门在哭泣, 城市在失眠 in adjacent
  sentences: each crossing was individually cheap, together they are a
  tic (see `literary-strategy-controller` on saturation).
- **The stolen agency** — the crossing displaces a character's action:
  if the character should 推门 but the door is given the hesitation, the
  event's responsibility moved without a scene reason.

## Minimal pair

> 走廊尽头，灯一盏一盏熄了，像有人在关。
>
> 走廊尽头，灯一盏一盏熄了，整栋楼在收拢它的眼睛。

The first is a comparison (simile, explicit). The second is a crossing that
claims sentience for the building. Both may work; the second's cost is the
claim that the building sees. If the scene is about surveillance, the
borrowed perception is the point and the cost pays. If the scene is a power
outage, the first is cheaper and nothing is lost.
