# Coercion and Result

A productive predicate can grant a noun a temporary event capacity it does not usually possess.

> 视线被雪浮起一截。

`视线` becomes something measurable and vertically displaced; `雪` becomes a cause acting on perceptual geometry. The phrase works only if the reader reconstructs the intended event before stopping to litigate the collocation.

## Coercion test

1. What new event capacity is granted?
2. Is there a concrete scene relation that supports it?
3. Does a nearby measure/result phrase stabilize the crossing?
4. Can the reader predict what changed?

If the answer is only “the phrase is surprising,” it is not enough.

## Result complements and result verbs

Result marking is valuable when the endpoint is the scene's information. A verb that already carries result can make an explanatory clause redundant.
