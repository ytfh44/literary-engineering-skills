# Japanese-Derived Viewpoint Engineering

## Use when

Japanese makes some viewpoint decisions unusually cheap and visible. Use those mechanisms as a laboratory for controlling **where the reader stands, whose access matters, and how attention changes**.

## Do not use when

Do not treat `ふと`, `ちらり`, `じっと`, and similar words as a formal “viewpoint adverb” grammatical class. Do not import Japanese ellipsis or deictic choices literally into another language.

## Core test

**Keep the event constant. Change the viewpoint carrier. If the reader now stands somewhere else, the carrier is doing narrative work.**

## Workflow

1. Fix the objective event before touching viewpoint.
2. Locate the deictic center: here/there, toward/away, giver/receiver, perceiver/observed.
3. Track who remains implicit across omitted subjects.
4. Identify the attention mode: accidental notice, glance, sustained fixation, diffuse looking, threshold realization.
5. Change one carrier and compare reader position.
6. If writing another language, restate the operation abstractly and find a native carrier. See **Transfer**.

## Working operations

- **center** — choose the narrative here/now;
- **orient** — encode toward/away relative to that center;
- **empathize** — privilege one participant's benefit/access;
- **sustain** — let omitted arguments inherit a stable viewpoint;
- **sample** — glance or briefly acquire information;
- **lock** — sustain attention;
- **trigger** — mark unplanned attention or sudden recognition.

## Minimal pair

Japanese source:

> ふと窓を見ると、雪が降っていた。

Neutralized paraphrase:

> 窓を見る。雪が降っていた。

`ふと` does not add a new object. It changes the entry path into the perception: unplanned attention becomes part of the event.

## Counterexamples

- Adding `ふと` to every discovery does not create viewpoint; it creates a tic.
- English “suddenly” is not a universal translation of `ふと`; sudden event onset and unplanned attention are different variables.

## Transfer

Treat Japanese as a high-resolution map of viewpoint, not as a closed toolbox. Abstract `ふと窓を見る` to “attention turns toward the window without prior intention.” Chinese may realize that with an action such as `抬眼`, an adverb, or event order; English may use a perception verb or clause timing; French may lean on perception/appearance predicates or discourse order. The target sentence succeeds when the reader inherits the same attention dynamics, even if no word corresponds to `ふと`. Literal resemblance is optional; viewpoint equivalence is not.

**Transfer the operation, not the costume.**
