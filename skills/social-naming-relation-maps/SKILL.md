# Social Naming and Relation Maps

## Use when

A single naming act is a frame switch (see `naming-and-address-engineering`).
Across a whole work, naming acts accumulate into a map — and the map is a
structure of its own: who may call whom what, who refuses to, and when the
map changes.

## Do not use when

Do not police naming consistency as an error-correction task. Consistency
is a default, not a law: the map exists to be redrawn. Do not collect
name-switches as decoration; every switch is either structure or noise,
and the map decides which.

## Core test

**If every naming act in the work were plotted, what social structure would
the plot show?**

The map has three layers:

- **permissions** — who is allowed to use which name for whom (title,
  kin term, nickname, first name, none);
- **asymmetries** — a calls B by first name while B calls A 主任: the
  asymmetry is the relationship;
- **arcs** — names that change over time, tracking a relationship's
  trajectory: 李叔 → 老李 → 李建国 is a biography in three names.

## Workflow

1. Collect every naming act for the work's central referents (per scene).
2. Plot the map: who uses which name for whom, and when.
3. Find asymmetries and note what they encode (power, intimacy, refusal,
   role performance).
4. Find arcs: names that changed, and the scene where each change became
   legal.
5. Check whether each change was earned: the frame must have moved
   (knowledge, relation, distance, relevance — see
   `naming-and-address-engineering`).
6. Check consistency where nothing changed: a fixed name across a decade
   is itself a statement (the relation did not move, or one party refused
   to let it).
7. Use the map: when a new scene needs a naming act, choose from the
   referent's legal set — or deliberately break the law of the map as the
   scene's event.

## Minimal pair

> 她叫他李叔，他叫她小王，十年没变。
>
> 她叫他李叔。第七年，他第一次说："叫我老李就行。"

The first is a static map: the asymmetry is the relationship. The second
is an arc: the permission changed, and the offer is the event. Neither is
"better naming"; the map decides what the names do.

> 散会后，他叫住她："王主任。" 她没回头。
>
> 散会后，他叫住她："小王。" 她没回头。

Same refusal, opposite maps. The first keeps institutional distance after
hours (the permission never existed); the second asserts a familiarity the
relationship has not earned (the permission was refused). The reader reads
the relationship from the map, not from the dialogue alone.

## Counterexamples

- A formal register may require titles even when intimacy exists (主任 in
  front of colleagues); role performance is a legal map state.
- A character may misname deliberately as a conflict in miniature
  (refusing a new title, using a dead nickname); the misname is a move,
  not an error.
- A work may keep one naming law throughout (everyone uses first names)
  as a statement about its world; the uniform map is the design.

## Rule strength

**Structure tests.** No naming-consistency mandates, no "avoid repetition"
advice (names are not pronouns), no synonym-variety rules. The reliable
operations are map drawing, asymmetry reading, arc detection, and
permission-change testing. The map is reference, not prescription: any
state is legal if the work's relations justify it.

Return **map (who uses what for whom, when) -> asymmetries -> arcs ->
earned/uneamed changes -> one naming act the next scene can use (or
deliberately break)**.
