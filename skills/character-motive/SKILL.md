# Character Motive

## Use when

The character has traits but no generator. Build a stable rule that can
produce several surface labels under different objects and pressures — and
remember the rule is partial, not total.

## Do not use when

Do not confuse consistency with monotony. Motives can conflict, learn,
decay, or acquire exceptions. Do not force every behavior into one elegant
rule: a character compressed into a single decision function is a machine,
not a person.

## Core model

```text
stable motive + object/context -> behavior -> reader label
```

**The label is an observation, not a character parameter.**

A strong new scene may break the reader's model. It should not break the
character's generator without cause.

## Partial coherence

Behavior is generated by more than a motive:

```text
behavior =
  durable motives
  + habits
  + physiological state
  + learned scripts
  + beliefs
  + misbeliefs
  + role performance
  + social imitation
  + unresolved conflicts
  + path dependence
  + context
  + residual
```

The **residual is not explained by default.** Not every behavior must trace
to the generator. A character with one unexplained impulse is a person with
a history; a character whose every act is derivable is a theorem. When the
residual is left unexplained, do not fill it with a hidden motive — the
unexplained part is what makes the model a person rather than a decision
function.

## Workflow

1. Delete trait adjectives from the character sheet.
2. Write one or two durable priorities: debt, possession, fairness, status,
   curiosity, control, kinship, craft, survival, recognition.
3. Define the priority's **domain**. To whom or what does it apply? Where
   does it stop?
4. Place the same priority in unlike contexts.
5. Let readers infer an easy label from the first two outputs.
6. Introduce a case that violates the label but still follows the priority.
7. Repeat until the reader can predict the person without reducing them to
   one adjective.
8. Check the other factors: is the behavior partly habit, script, role, or
   imitation? Does anything stay residual? If nothing is residual, the
   model is too clean.

## Hard rules

- "Cruel but loves kittens" is not depth by itself.
- Do not use tenderness as automatic moral redemption.
- Do not make every contradiction secretly noble.
- Competence is local. A character can be brilliant about one recurring
  object and naive elsewhere.
- A character need not understand their own generator in theoretical
  language.
- Do not explain the residual: an unexplained preference, tic, or fear can
  stand as written.

## Minimal pair

Flat contrast:

> He is stingy, but once a year he gives a large anonymous donation.

Generated contrast:

> He refuses to split bills by "roughly half," yet spends freely to repay
> any debt he can name exactly.

The second pair can arise from one rule: **accounts must close cleanly**.
"Stingy" and "generous" are reader labels that fail on different
transactions.

Partial-coherence pair:

> Everything he does traces to his fear of abandonment.
>
> He hoards receipts, visits his mother every Sunday, and once let a
> stranger sleep in his car for no reason he can explain.

The first is a complete theory of the person — and therefore not a person.
The second leaves one act residual; the residual is the character.

## Counterexamples

- A genuinely impulsive character may lack one stable decision rule;
  instability itself can be causal if the triggers are legible.
- A sudden value change can be correct after a specific event that changes
  what the character believes, fears, or owes.
- Role performance can override motive for a scene (the boss who must fire
  a friend); the performance is behavior, not hypocrisy.
- A character who imitates another's voice or habits is borrowing a script;
  the borrowing is the character's act.

## Rule strength

**Low — counterexample-heavy.** No trait templates, no "flaw + virtue"
formulas, no hidden-motive requirement, no demand that every behavior
resolve into one rule. The reliable operations are generator/domain
construction, label-vs-parameter discipline, label-breaking sequence
design, and residual preservation. Every rule here is a hypothesis the
next scene may overturn.

## Return shape

Return **generator -> domain -> three predicted behaviors -> likely reader
labels -> scene that breaks each label -> what stays residual**. Keep the
generator shorter than the behavior list.
